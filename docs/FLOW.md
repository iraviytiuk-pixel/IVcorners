# User flow

## Screen 1 — Landing *(built)*

One fixed screen, no scroll. Answers three questions in under ten seconds: what is this, what do
I get, why trust it.

Copy still to apply: a subline under the headline carrying the factual offer ("A scaled floor
plan, a material palette, and every piece ready to buy. In minutes, with no designer in the
loop."), AIrena's intro rewritten toward value rather than backstory, and a credibility line
naming the portfolio.

## Screen 2 — The Brief *(built)*

The intake, and it does the explaining. No separate explainer page: a pitch screen spends the
enthusiasm that made someone click instead of using it.

AIrena opens with three sentences — what she's making, what they leave with, how long it takes —
then asks which room in the same breath. Room chips, then the shape picker, then dimensions.

Remaining questions to add: window and door placement, who lives here and how they use the room
(the psychology layer — the question a designer asks and software never does), what it should
feel like, style calibration via image pairs, budget band.

## Screen 3 — The Plan *(built, simplified)*

The plan draws itself: walls first, then furniture placing in sequence. The animation is proof
that something was computed rather than retrieved, and it covers the generation time.

AIrena narrates two or three decisions in a designer's voice — *"I've floated the sofa off the
wall so the room reads wider"* — each tied to something the customer said.

Do not let the user drag furniture. The moment the plan becomes editable it becomes their design
rather than Irena's, clearances break, and the product's authority evaporates. Offer two
alternatives, not a canvas.

## Screen 4 — The Look *(not built)*

The emotional payoff and the most screenshot-able screen. Material palette as the hero —
photographed samples, not swatches, in the style of `assets/palette-board.png`.

Render caution: a photoreal render of the customer's actual room with actual purchasable
furniture is the hardest thing in the product and the most likely to look wrong. For v1, a styled
mood composition from real product photography, or one hero render with the material board
carrying the weight.

## Screen 5 — The List *(not built)*

Where the design becomes actionable and where affiliate revenue lives. Every piece with real
photography, price, dimensions and a buy link, grouped as people shop — seating, surfaces, light,
soft layer — with a running total.

Each piece offers two or three alternatives at different price points that still fit the plan:
same footprint, same clearances, same palette. Every swap is another converting click.

The customer must leave with something durable — a PDF or saved link carrying plan, palette and
list. Without it the whole session evaporates when the tab closes.

## Progress labels

User-facing: **your brief · your plan · your look · your list**. The internal pillar names
(psychology, layout, style, shop) are for us. Customers want deliverables, not taxonomy.
